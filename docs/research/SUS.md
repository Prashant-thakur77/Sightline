# System Usability Scale (SUS)

Ten statements. The person answers each from 1 to 5. Use it once, right after the tasks and before the debrief.

## Instructions for verbal administration
Read this out:

"I will read ten short statements about the app you just used. After each one, say a number from 1 to 5. 1 means you strongly disagree. 5 means you strongly agree. 3 is in the middle. There are no right answers. Go with your first feeling. If a statement does not fit, say 3. I can read any statement again."

Rules for the moderator:
- Read each statement word for word, in order. Do not explain or add examples.
- Say the scale again after statement 1, 5 and 9.
- Write down the number. Do not react to it.
- If they ask what a word means, say "whatever it means to you".
- Ask for the answer to be a whole number. If they say "3 or 4", ask them to pick one.

## The statements
1. I think I would like to use this app often.
2. I found the app more complex than it needed to be.
3. I thought the app was easy to use.
4. I think I would need help from a technical person to use this app.
5. I found the different functions in the app fit together well.
6. I thought there was too much inconsistency in the app.
7. I think most people would learn to use this app very quickly.
8. I found the app very awkward to use.
9. I felt confident using the app.
10. I needed to learn a lot of things before I could get going with the app.

## Scoring
Odd items (1, 3, 5, 7, 9) are positive. Even items (2, 4, 6, 8, 10) are negative.

- Odd item contribution = answer - 1
- Even item contribution = 5 - answer
- SUS score = (sum of the ten contributions) x 2.5

The score runs from 0 to 100. It is not a percentage. Report the mean and the range across people, and say how many people. A score of about 68 is commonly treated as average. With a handful of people, treat the number as a rough signal only.

## Worked example
Answers: 1=4, 2=2, 3=4, 4=1, 5=5, 6=2, 7=4, 8=1, 9=4, 10=2
